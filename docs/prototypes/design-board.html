Ski Time

Skiers carve the hours and minutes into fresh snow. Each minute the old skiers ski out of frame, an avalanche wipes the slope clean, and a new group drops in from the top right to draw the next time. The preview below uses the watch's real resolution and its 64-color palette.

Layout. Sky, sun and mountains fill the triangle above the ridge. Trees line the ridge. Hours sit above minutes, and the minutes row is shifted left along the fall line, as in your sketch.

Untouched snow. The strip under the drift line holds three complication slots, like constellation. Skiers and the avalanche never mark it.

Tracks. Tracks appear only on the digit strokes. The ski-in and ski-out lines leave no marks. Turn on the overlay to see them.

Settings. Controls marked Setting will be options on the watch's settings page. Without a leading zero, a one-digit hour sits over the minutes' ones digit, and that hour gets one skier fewer.

The minute change

Four moments, 20:46 → 20:47

0.6 s

Ski out

Each resting skier turns from the hockey-stop pose back to the fall line and skis off the bottom left.

2.0 s

Avalanche

A crack opens under the ridge at the top right. A ragged wall of rolling billows then pours down-left, with debris tumbling ahead of it and a shadow thrown onto the snow. It buries the old tracks and stops at the drift line.

5.0 s

Drop in

New skiers enter from the right edge a few beats apart. They leave grooves only while they are on a digit stroke.

Rest

Hold

Each skier stops at the bottom of their digit, turned across the slope, and waits there for the next minute.

Curly script numerals

Digit trails

Every stroke ends low on the digit, pointing somewhere a skier can leave from. The 0, 4 and 8 use two skiers so that nobody has to climb. The 0 and 8 pairs cross like a real figure-8 run, with the second skier a beat behind the first. The trail rating shows how much each stroke climbs.

About 8 px tall on Emery

The skier

Twin skis, a jacket dot and a helmet. The skis follow the smoothed direction of travel, so the rotation reads clearly even at this size. Each digit's skier has their own jacket color.

Pebble GColor names

Palette

Every pixel snaps to the watch's 64 colors. The sky gradient and the soft snow shadows use a 4×4 ordered dither, the same approach as analog-adapt.

Settled in round 2

Decisions

  1. Chalet: bottom right, three-quarter view

    The gable end faces the viewer and the side wall runs off to the right, with glowing windows and a chimney on the side roof. The skis that leaned on the front wall are gone.

  2. Timing: about 5 s

    A full change takes 4 to 5.2 s, depending on the digits. Ski out takes about 1 s and the avalanche about 1 s. The new skiers drop in 0.2 s apart.

  3. Track texture: a setting

    People choose twin grooves or solid on the settings page. The default is twin grooves.

  4. Clock: a setting

    12 or 24 hour, and leading zero on or off. A one-digit hour sits over the minutes' ones digit.

  5. Screens: Emery and Basalt

    Round screens (Chalk, Gabbro) are out of scope for now.

  6. Digit shapes: approved

    All ten trails stay as drawn.

  7. Scenery: a denser tree line

    Two rows of pines run along the ridge from about a fifth of the way in. That leaves the left side of the sky open for the mountains.
